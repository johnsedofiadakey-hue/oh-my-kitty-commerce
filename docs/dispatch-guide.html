Dispatch & Parcel Tracking

How orders get packed, handed over, and tracked — and how the system stops the same order being sent out twice.

Who this is for — the owner, and anyone who packs orders.

Where to find it — Admin portal → Dispatch in the left menu.

Version — September 2026

Section 1Why this exists

When an order is paid for online, the money is already settled. The thing still at risk is the stock — and the way it gets lost is by packing the same order twice.

It happens easily. Two people look at the same list of paid orders. Both see one that needs packing. Both pack it. Two parcels go out, one payment came in. Or one person packs an order, gets interrupted, comes back an hour later and doesn't remember.

Before this change, nothing in the system recorded the physical act of packing. The order list showed what was paid, not what had already been put in a box.

The core rule

One order can only have one parcel out at a time. Once an order is packed, trying to pack it again is refused — and the message says who packed it and when.

If someone tries to pack it again

They'll get a message like: "OMK-OVMZNAUA is already packed — parcel OMK-OVMZNAUA-P1, by Ama on 18/09/2026 14:32. Don't pack it again." Nothing is sent twice, and it's clear who to ask.

What else it gives you

  • A pick list for every order, with sets broken down into the actual items to fetch.
  • A printed slip that stays on the parcel, so anyone can see at a glance it's already done.
  • A record of who packed what, and when.
  • Returns tracking — what came back, why, and how often.
  • Safe delegation. Staff can now pack and hand over without being able to quietly undo anything.

Section 2The journey of an order

Every paid online order moves left to right through these stages. It cannot skip backwards without a manager's override and a written reason.

To packpaid, not yet boxed
Packedin a box, on the bench
Outwith the rider
Delivereddone

With one branch: if a parcel comes back undelivered, it becomes a Return. That's the only thing that frees the order to be packed and sent again.

The Dispatch page has one tab per stage, in the order you work through them.

Section 3Packing an order, step by step

Most important habit

Tap Pack before you walk to the shelf — not after you've filled the box. The moment you tap it, the order is locked to you, and nobody else can pack it behind your back. If you pack first and tap later, that gap is exactly where two people collide.

1

Open Dispatch → To pack

Every paid order without a parcel is listed, newest first, with the customer's name, their delivery method and location, and the order value.

2

Tap Pack — check the list that appears

A panel opens showing exactly what goes in this parcel, with a total item count. Read this before you fetch anything.

If the order contains a set, it is broken down into the individual products inside it — you'll see "6× Feminine Wash · from Chronic Infection Set", not just the set name. This is the difference between packing it right and short-shipping someone.

3

Tap "Pack it"

The order is now locked. It disappears from To pack and appears under Packed. The confirmation tells you the parcel number, for example OMK-OVMZNAUA-P1.

4

Go to Packed and print the slip

Find the parcel, tap Slip. It prints on the same small thermal printer as your receipts. Use it as your pick list while you fill the box, then tape it to the outside.

5

Fill the box and seal it

The slip stays on the parcel. That's the visible signal to everyone else that this one is finished.

What's on it

  • Parcel number — the order number plus -P1. A second parcel for the same order would be -P2.
  • Packed by and when — so any question has a name attached.
  • Where it's going — customer, phone, location, and which delivery method they chose.
  • Every item, with sets already broken down. Anything that came from a set says so.
  • Total item count — a quick check against what's in the box before sealing.
Watch for this line

If the slip says "REPEAT SEND" at the bottom, this order has been sent before and came back. Double-check the address before it goes out again.

Section 5Handing parcels to a rider

The Packed tab has two separate lists, deliberately kept apart.

Ready to go out

Parcels waiting for a rider. Works for a whole Tuesday batch or a single urgent handover — the process is the same.

1

Tick the parcels going out

Or use Select all if the rider is taking everything.

2

Type who's taking them

The rider or courier's name. Optional, but it's what lets you trace a missing parcel back to a person later.

3

Tap Dispatch

All ticked parcels move to Out, and each customer gets their "on the way" text message automatically.

Waiting for collection

Pickup orders sit in their own list. They're packed and ready, but nobody is coming to fetch them on a schedule — the customer comes in when they come in. Keeping them separate stops them cluttering the rider queue.

When the customer collects, tap Delivered on that parcel.

The working assumption

Once a parcel is out, treat it as delivered unless it comes back. You don't need to confirm each one individually — just record the ones that return. That keeps the daily work light while still giving you an accurate count.

Section 6When things don't go to plan

An item is missing — you can't pack it

Don't just leave the order sitting there. Tap Can't pack and write what's blocking it, for example "out of 100ml bottles until Thursday".

The reason then shows on that order in the list, so the next person reads it instead of walking to the shelf and finding the same gap. When you do finally pack it, the hold clears itself.

A parcel comes back

On the Out tab, find the parcel and tap Came back. Write why — unreachable, refused, wrong address. Leave "Put the items back into stock" ticked unless the goods are damaged.

The stock goes back automatically. If the parcel contained a set, the individual products inside it are what get restored, not the set.

Only record a return once the parcel is physically back

Recording a return is the one action that unlocks an order to be packed and sent again. Doing it before the box is actually in your hands defeats the whole system. This is why only you and managers can do it — packing staff cannot.

Sending an order again after a return

Once a parcel is marked returned, the order reappears under To pack marked reship. Packing it asks one extra question: why are you sending it again?

The new parcel is numbered -P2, the reason is recorded against it, and it's listed under Returns → Sent a second time. The slip prints with a REPEAT SEND warning.

Something was marked wrong

If an order was marked delivered by mistake, only an owner or manager can move it back, and the system requires a written reason. That reason is stored permanently in the audit log. It's meant to be a small amount of friction — going backwards is exactly how an order gets sent twice.

Why returns are restricted

Marking a parcel returned is the only way to free an order for a second send. If packing staff could do it themselves, someone could mark a parcel returned that never came back, pack it again, and take the second one. Keeping that one action with a manager is what makes everything else safe to delegate.

Every action above is written to the audit log with a name and a timestamp — including reasons given for holds, returns, repeat sends, and reversals.

Section 8What to watch

The Returns tab carries the two numbers worth checking regularly.

Return rate

Shown at the top of the Returns tab as a percentage of everything dispatched. A few returns are normal in rider delivery. A rising rate usually means addresses are being taken down badly at checkout, or customers aren't being reached before the rider sets off.

Sent a second time

Every order that went out more than once, with the reason given each time. A short list is ordinary. A growing one is worth investigating — either deliveries are failing repeatedly, or parcels are being recorded as returned when they weren't.

A quick weekly check

Open Returns once a week. If the rate is steady and the repeat-send list is short, dispatch is healthy and you can leave it alone.

Common questions

Can I still pack an order the old way, without a parcel?

No. Packing always creates a parcel now. That's what makes the double-send check possible.

What about orders that were already out before this was switched on?

They finish exactly as they are. They don't appear in To pack and can't be accidentally re-packed — the system recognises they've already gone.

Does the customer see any of this?

Only the text message they already got — that their order is on the way, or ready for collection. Parcel numbers and slips are internal.

What if the printer isn't working?

Packing still works — printing is separate. But without the slip on the box you lose the visual marker, so print it as soon as the printer is back, or write the parcel number on the box by hand.

Can two people pack different orders at the same time?

Yes. The lock is per order, not per person. Only packing the same order twice is blocked.
